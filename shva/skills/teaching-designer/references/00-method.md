# 00 - The Method (arc, section anatomy, revision recap)

Bundled copy of the canonical method. The wiki source of truth is `maps/executive-teaching-keynote-method.md`. Pair with `concepts/work-charts-for-agentic-organizations.md` (content frames) and `concepts/shiva-persona-and-taste.md` (voice).

## The shape of a good keynote doc (design backward from this)

- One idea, scaled across altitudes. Pick a single contrarian idea and trace it through Individual, then Team, then Organization. The same idea at three resolutions, not three topics.
- A running thread carried through every section, shown as a small recurring chip (for example "Where does the human stand").
- A wrapper analogy opened cold (sounds nothing like the topic) and closed at higher resolution. The bridge lands in retrospect.
- A memorable named principle per section, plus an end revision recap listing all of them with a short "Apply it" action each.
- Light, not dense. One analogy, one short clip, one discussion prompt per act.
- Experience before explanation. A micro-exercise or poll lets the room discover the point before it is named.
- Evidence with honesty: real citations, quotable lines, and a caveat held in the facilitation notes.

## The four-beat rhythm inside an act

1. Contrarian opening or hook that disarms, then bridges to the point.
2. Interactive experience (a poll, a micro-exercise, a clip) where the room discovers before naming.
3. The idea named, with the framework as light garnish and one analogy from their world.
4. Reflection that hands off to the next act and advances the running thread.

## The scaled-spine arc (the backbone)

- Pick one contrarian idea. XLRI example: "AI cross-skills, it does not upskill." Value moves from depth in one silo to breadth and judgment across silos.
- Scale it across three altitudes, one act each (about 20 minutes): Individual, then Team, then Organization.
- Run a single thread through all of them. XLRI example: "Where does the human stand", moving from a link in a human chain, to a director of one AI, to the decision points of a tree of agents.
- Wrap it in one analogy. XLRI example: the electrified factory (around 1900) that bolted motors onto the old steam-era floor and gained almost nothing for twenty years, until the floor was redesigned around the motor. Open cold, close on it at higher resolution.

## Section anatomy (each section carries)

- A named principle banner at the top: a short, sticky, plain maxim with a memorable name.
- The teaching script (collapsible in the HTML, spoken word, delivery cues in italics).
- Evidence where useful: a real statistic, a verbatim line, attribution, and a caveat in the facilitation notes.
- A micro-exercise with nudge scaffolds and a worked example (see `03-pedagogy-and-taste.md`).
- A before/after diagram where structure changes (handoffs collapsing, a chain becoming a tree).
- Discussion prompts (nudge questions) with a facilitation note.

## The revision recap (always at the end)

List every named principle in order. For each: the name, the one-line rule, and an "Apply it" action. It doubles as a printable takeaway. XLRI example set: The Floor Not the Motor; The 70 Percent Rule; Wider Not Deeper; The Vanishing Handoff; Two Piles; Stand at the Decision Points.

## Course outline mode

When the request is a multi-session program, output a table: session number, session name, one-line contrarian hook, the experiential activity, the frameworks (as garnish), and the learning outcome. Sequence foundations to applications to synthesis. Each session still obeys the four-beat rhythm.
