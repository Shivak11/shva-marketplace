# 04 - Writing rules (hard, non-negotiable)

Shiva has corrected these repeatedly. They apply to every artifact this skill produces. Canonical source: `concepts/shiva-persona-and-taste.md` and the feedback memory on his writing style.

## The hard rules

- **No em-dashes and no en-dashes.** Use periods, colons, commas, parentheses, or "is". This includes time ranges: write "0 to 12 min", never "0-12" with a dash glyph. Grep the artifact for the en-dash and em-dash characters before declaring it done.
- **No stylish or GPT-cloud language.** Drop cute parallelism ("one half is the machine's, one half is yours"). Say it plainly ("separating execution from judgment").
- **Hints and helper text are complete sentences** in plain language a casual reader understands, not terse fragments.
- **Add a short plain description of what a thing is** before diving into it.
- **Flag uncertain terminology.** If you coin a term for a visual (for example "work tree"), say once that it is your label and name the established term (tree of agents, orchestrator-worker, Work Chart).

## The author-name rule (artifact-dependent, do not get this wrong)

The flat rule for participant-facing artifacts (worksheets, slides, handouts) is: no author or source names anywhere. It is Shiva's synthesis, not a reading list. Strip every attribution.

The facilitator's own teaching doc is different. It MAY carry citations, but only in two contained places: dedicated framework and evidence cards, and a sources block at the end. The spoken script itself keeps names as light verbal garnish at most ("a 758-consultant study", "the head of Nvidia put it bluntly"). Never plaster citations across the script, and never put them on a slide the room sees.

Rule of thumb: if a participant will read it, no names. If only Shiva (the facilitator) reads it, names live in cards and a sources block, not in the prose he speaks.

## Boxes and proportions (when an artifact has fillable space)

Field and answer boxes are proportional to what is asked. A "draw a 5 to 9 step workflow" prompt gets a large area, not two lines. This matters mostly for worksheets (see the worksheet-generator skill), but applies to any printed exercise space here too.

## Check before done

Grep for em-dash and en-dash glyphs. Confirm no author names sit on participant surfaces or inside the spoken script. Confirm every coined term is flagged.
