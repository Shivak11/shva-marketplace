# 03 - Pedagogy and taste

The laws that make a session land, and the reusable patterns.

## Pedagogy laws

- Experience before explanation. Never define a framework before the room has felt the thing. Use a poll, a micro-exercise, or a clip first, then name it.
- Intellectual tension precedes resolution. Comfort is not the goal. Open a gap, then close it.
- One idea, scaled. Trace a single contrarian idea across Individual, Team, Organization. Do not spread across three unrelated topics.
- Analogies from their world. Cricket, Bollywood, a grocery store, a factory floor. The analogy carries the idea; the framework is garnish.
- Make the obvious non-obvious, then re-obvious at higher resolution. The wrapper analogy returns at the end with more meaning.

## The named principle per section

Every section earns one sticky, named, plain-language maxim. It sits in a banner at the top of the section and reappears in the revision recap. Keep the name short and concrete (The Floor Not the Motor, Two Piles, Stand at the Decision Points). The maxim is one sentence, no jargon, no author names.

## The revision recap

At the very end, list all the named principles in order. For each: the name, a one-line rule, and an "Apply it" action that a participant can do on Monday. This is the takeaway handout.

## Micro-exercise patterns (validated)

Keep exercises physical, fast, and grounded in the participant's own workplace. Each has nudge scaffolds and a faint worked example.

### Pattern A: the JD rewrite (breadth and judgment)

Pairs take a real job description and rewrite three lines to reward breadth and direction, not only depth. Nudge scaffolds: add one responsibility that rewards directing work across functions the person is not expert in; add one line that rewards judgment (catching confident wrong output); turn one "X years in Y" into "can learn and direct Z"; cut one line that rewards depth for its own sake. Show a before/after model line after they try.

### Pattern B: the task decomposition (Jesuthasan and Boudreau, by hand)

Pairs take a real role and list 6 to 8 core tasks (deconstruct). Tag each task E for execution that could move to a machine, A for augment where human and AI work together, D for a decision never handed over. The E and A piles become the automation or agent layer; the D pile is the redesigned human role. The hard question: is the D pile, on its own, a whole and respected role? This is the four steps (deconstruct, value the task, match the automation, reconstruct) done physically, and it sets up the org-level payoff.

## Evidence cards

Where a claim needs proof: show the real statistic large, the verbatim line in quotes, the attribution, and a plain reframe. Keep the caveat in the facilitation notes. Pair a famous-but-contestable figure with a larger corroborating study as backup.

## Diagrams (before and after)

Where structure changes, draw it: a relay of handoffs becoming one augmented role; a chain of humans becoming a tree of agents. Build as Mermaid, validate before embedding, keep labels short, and write a one-line caption that states the shift in plain words.

## Voice

Conversational, provocative, lucid, never didactic. Independent and forward-thinking. Humor when natural. Avoid cliche, generic academic language, and excessive encouragement. See `concepts/shiva-persona-and-taste.md`.
