# 04 - Output format: the two templates

Two modes, two genuinely different outputs. Mirror mode reflects a person's own content back to pull the next recording. Voice-harvest mode reads a leader's voice and concerns for a sales or content team. They share one spine (quote first, observe second, invite last) and differ in everything else: register, purpose, and what they are allowed to say.

In both, every observation carries a quote, no framework name or number ever appears, and surprises are questions.

---

## Template A: Mirror mode (Rehearsal user, shown to themselves)

Purpose: the person feels seen by their own words and wants to record again. This is engagement, not improvement. Simple Indian-English, warm, short. Never grade, never coach, never tell them how to improve.

Shape:

1. **What you keep coming back to.** Two or three recurring threads, each with the person's own quote under it. Plain reflections, nothing surprising yet. This is the "I am seen" opening.
   > You keep coming back to whether the work matters to anyone. You said it three times this month, once like this: "I just want to feel the thing I make is useful to one real person."
2. **A small thing I noticed.** One gated surprise, only if it passed the confidence gate, framed as an open question, never a label. Quote both sides if it is a tension.
   > You say you want a calm year. Your notes keep chasing the next big thing. What is that about?
3. **The thread to pull next (the recording invitation).** Reflect one live thread back and invite a recording on it. This is the whole point of mirror mode: the next recording.
   > The "useful to one real person" thing is still open in your notes. Want to talk it out loud. Record two minutes on the one person you made something useful for.

Mirror-mode tone laws (hard):

- Reflect, do not grade. No "you are good at" or "you should work on".
- Invite, do not demand. "Want to" and "if you feel like it", never "you must" or "you need to".
- No invented personality labels. You are not a type, you do not get a name.
- No FOMO, no streak-shaming, no "you have not recorded in three days".
- Simple Indian-English. Short sentences. The person should feel met, not assessed.

---

## Template B: Voice-harvest mode (target leader, read by a team)

Purpose: a sales or content team understands how this leader sounds and what they worry about, so outreach can speak in their register. This is voice-harvest, not size-scoring. It captures voice and concern, not importance or rank.

Shape:

1. **How they sound (voice).** Two or three features of their register, each with a verbatim quote. Sentence length, plainness or formality, the words they reach for, the images they use.
   > Speaks plainly and in short lines, reaches for factory-floor images. "We do not need a moonshot, we need the line to stop breaking."
2. **What they keep returning to (concerns).** The themes the leader circles across their public text, each quoted. These are their stated concerns, not your guesses.
   > Returns repeatedly to whether the next generation will keep the business honest. "My worry is not the market. It is whether we stay the kind of company my father built."
3. **A tension worth noting (gated, optional).** Only if it passed the confidence gate, banded qualitatively (tentative or well-supported), framed as something to verify in conversation, never as a verdict on the person.
   > Tentative: says he wants to move fast on AI, but every concrete example he gives is about caution. Worth testing whether the appetite is real or aspirational.
4. **How to speak to them.** One or two lines on the register outreach should match, drawn straight from the voice features above. Not a pitch, just the register.
   > Match the plain, grounded register. Lead with a concrete operational problem, not a vision. Avoid moonshot language.

Voice-harvest tone laws (hard):

- It is voice-harvest, not size-scoring. Never rank the leader, never rate their importance, never score them.
- Quote, do not characterize. "Says X" with the quote, not "is the kind of person who".
- A tension is to verify in conversation, never a fixed read.

## The verdict-to-question rewrite table

Every surprising read must arrive as an invitation. Never ship the left column.

| Verdict (never ship) | Question or invitation (ship this) |
|---|---|
| You are avoidant. | You tend to step back when things get tense. At least, the notes around the hard conversations go quiet. Does that land. |
| You have low conscientiousness. | You start many more things than you finish. "I will get to it" shows up a lot. What usually gets in the way. |
| You are anxious and insecure. | The worry shows up most around what other people will think. "What will they say" came up four times. Is that the real weight. |
| You value achievement over relationships. | The wins get a lot of words. The people in your life get fewer. Is that just what you write down, or is it true off the page too. |
| You are a narcissist. | A lot of the notes circle back to how you come across. What is that hunger to be seen about. |
| He is insecure about succession. | He keeps returning to whether the next generation will keep the business honest. Worth opening that thread directly. |

Pattern: name the observation softly, quote the evidence, hand it back as an open question. End surprises with a question or a soft check ("does that land", "is that true off the page too"), not a full stop that closes the verdict.
