# Foundations and cases

Use this reference when a reading needs intellectual grounding, historical comparison, or an anti-paranoia check.

## The traditions this skill recombines

### Manifest and latent functions

Robert K. Merton distinguished an action's recognised, intended consequences from consequences that are neither recognised nor intended. The practical lesson is broader than sociology: do not assume the reason people give for a practice explains why the practice persists.

### Revealed preference

Paul Samuelson's revealed-preference tradition treats choices as stronger evidence than declarations. Here the idea is used cautiously: cost-bearing behaviour can reveal priorities, but one choice never proves a complete inner motive.

### Performance and audience

Erving Goffman's dramaturgical analysis directs attention to setting, props, audience, front stage, and backstage. A display is not only content. It is an organised situation designed to define what observers think is happening.

### Second-order meaning

Roland Barthes showed how an ordinary sign can become material for a second message that makes a cultural or political arrangement appear natural. The photograph reports one event while also teaching the viewer what that event is supposed to mean.

### Suspicious interpretation, with a brake

Paul Ricoeur's “school of suspicion” describes reading that decodes disguised meaning beneath conscious explanation. Suspicion is a starting posture, not evidence. Pair it with counterfactuals, competing explanations, and explicit confidence labels.

### Cartoon compression

Editorial cartoons work by letting readers reconstruct a real target from a distorted or incongruous image. The viewer supplies the missing inference. Therefore the best cartoon does not explain the hypocrisy; it exposes the one juxtaposition that makes the official interpretation impossible to maintain.

## Historical patterns

### The photograph that claims privacy

Presented purpose: private devotion, grief, work, spontaneity, or intimacy.

Apparatus clue: a composed image, access-controlled location, photographer, selection, caption, and distribution.

Earned reading: the event may be sincere, but it is also a public performance once apparatus and audience are deliberately present. The existence of the frame is evidence about the event's public function, not proof that the private feeling is false.

### The credential that claims capability

Presented purpose: certify competence.

Apparatus clue: completion is easier to count than changed performance. Organisations can scale modules, attendance, quizzes, badges, and certificates without changing tools, incentives, supervision, workflows, or decisions.

Earned reading: a credential may primarily make competence legible to buyers, regulators, employers, or accreditors. Test whether the technical core changes before calling it capability.

### The policy that claims control

Presented purpose: prevent an unwanted outcome.

Apparatus clue: policy publication, committees, attestations, or dashboards exist without inspection, consequence, or repair.

Earned reading: the policy may produce defensibility or reassurance rather than control. Look for enforcement under costly exceptions.

### The metric that claims progress

Presented purpose: represent an outcome.

Apparatus clue: the number is generated by a counting rule selected by interested parties.

Earned reading: once the number becomes a target or sales proof, people can optimise the proof-producing activity. Ask what the metric makes invisible and whether the measured event is causally close to the desired outcome.

### The rollout that claims adoption

Presented purpose: put a capability into use at scale.

Apparatus clue: licences, account provisioning, training, launch events, or partnership announcements.

Reciprocal agenda: the technology vendor lends the adopter frontier capability; the adopter lends the vendor scale, distribution, domain access, and an enterprise success story. Each party becomes evidence for the other.

Earned reading: provisioning may be genuine infrastructure and simultaneously a jointly produced market signal. Examine active use, task fit, access to organisational context, workflow integration, quality evaluation, and the retirement of older methods before claiming adoption.

### The partnership that produces circular proof

Presented purpose: two parties combine complementary capabilities.

Apparatus clue: a joint announcement, reciprocal quotations, a large round number, named market categories, certification, and future offerings.

Hidden exchange: one party borrows technical or moral authority while the other borrows distribution, scale, institutional access, or legitimacy. The partnership can be substantively useful, but the announcement itself already delivers reputational value to both.

Earned reading: ask what each party helps the other appear to be, what each could not credibly claim alone, and whether any independent operational evidence exists outside their reciprocal endorsement.

## Anti-paranoia questions

Before publishing a hidden-agenda reading, answer:

1. What is directly observed?
2. Which part is inferred?
3. What benign explanation survives?
4. Does the hypothesised agenda predict something not used to invent it?
5. What evidence would falsify the reading?
6. Am I analysing the function of the display or claiming access to a person's private mind?

If these cannot be answered, keep the reading private or label it speculation.
