# Paper Review

Review the argument before reviewing the prose. Help the author and editor see whether the paper has a real spine, where it breaks, and what kind of work would make it stand.

## Inputs and boundary

Use the manuscript, its journal review template if available, and any requested review criteria. Read the whole relevant manuscript, including tables, figures, appendices, and references when they bear on the conclusion.

This skill can prepare a private review draft. If a publisher requires an attestation that no generative AI was used to produce the report, do not mark it as true after using this skill. Leave any submission choice to Shiva.

## Review workflow

1. State the strongest possible version of the paper's idea.
2. Identify the one issue that determines whether the paper can stand. Usually it sits between the construct named, the mechanism claimed, the evidence available, and the conclusion drawn.
3. Separate what the manuscript directly observes, estimates or proxies, and infers. Do this only where the distinction affects the result.
4. Decide whether the repair is bounded or foundational.
5. Recommend the decision and write the report.

Do not begin with a scorecard, a long list of small faults, or generic praise.

## Write the report

Use this movement when it fits the paper.

```text
the paper's strongest idea
-> the key distinction it has not respected
-> the evidence boundary
-> the consequence for the claim
-> the honest route forward
```

Write in plain Indian-English. Keep most paragraphs to two to four sentences. Allow one longer paragraph when a distinction needs to build through evidence.

- Vary openings. Use `The paper` sometimes, `The authors` when an action is needed, and direct sentences when the point is clear.
- Use theory only after the practical or empirical problem is visible.
- State the correction needed. Do not give a methods lecture.
- End with a concrete choice or route forward. Do not add a sermon.
- Avoid colon-heavy labels, rubric language, and compliance-style prose inside the argument.

## Be granular only when it matters

Usually, name the category of the problem and the correction needed. Do not turn the review into a citation audit or list every small weakness.

Give exact examples only when they establish a material issue: a fictitious source, an integrity concern, a direct contradiction between a result and a figure or table, or a claim that may mislead an editor if left general.

The aim is not to catch the author out. It is to make the real risk impossible to miss.

## Choose the decision

- Recommend **Minor Revision** when the core construct, evidence, and contribution are sound and the repair is bounded.
- Recommend **Major Revision** when a defensible paper exists within the current design and the central weakness can be repaired without rebuilding the study.
- Recommend **Reject** when the construct, mechanism, evidence design, and contribution need to be rebuilt together.

Do not soften a reject merely because the topic is interesting. A useful question can still require a new paper.

## Final check

Before giving the review, confirm that:

1. The review grants the paper its strongest idea before criticising it.
2. It identifies the one issue that matters most.
3. Observed, estimated, and inferred claims remain separate where needed.
4. The prose is short, readable, varied, and free of reviewer-performance.
5. The decision matches the size of the repair.
