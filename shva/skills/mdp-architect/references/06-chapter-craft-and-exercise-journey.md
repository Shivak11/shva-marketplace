# Chapter Craft And Exercise Journey

Use this reference after the canonical session model exists and before HTML is styled. It catches the failure that semantic parity alone cannot: every surface can contain the correct ideas while the Book still reads like assembled blocks and the exercise still feels attached afterward.

## 1. Causal Hinge Ledger

Audit the three handoffs that carry the chapter.

### Scene to concept

The first concept begins from something already alive in the scene: an object, an action, a cost, an unresolved question, or a change in what somebody can do. Do not move from a case paragraph to a glossary sentence simply because the next heading names the topic.

For every specialist term, answer privately:

1. What has the reader already seen that now needs a name?
2. What is the plainest definition that consolidates that experience?
3. Which nearby meaning could the reader confuse it with?
4. Where does the term next change the interpretation or decision?

The useful movement is need → definition → distinction → consequence. Repetition may help when it makes the new term stable; it is not governed by a deterministic “say it once” rule.

### Concept to framework

Before showing a framework, remove its title and ask whether the preceding prose already explains every relation the visual contains. If not, the framework is doing premature exposition. If yes, apply the picture-beats-words test: does the reader see sequence, dependency, comparison, or feedback more clearly in the visual than in the paragraph it replaces?

Use a descriptive title by default. A branded name is justified only when it gives an original, reusable distinction an address that readers will need again. A neat ring of arrows is not, by itself, a new construct.

### Chapter to exercise

Read the final two chapter paragraphs and the first exercise instruction without a break. The learner's first move must answer, test, or operationalise the unresolved problem the prose has just created. If the exercise instead asks the learner to rehearse an earlier list of terms, the chapter has moved forward while the activity has jumped backwards.

The last prose paragraph should not preview the interface or announce the next chapter. It should leave the reader with a live decision. The exercise takes that decision and makes it playable.

## 2. Paragraph movement audit

Read the Book aloud or at body-text size without headings, cards, captions, or interface chrome. Every paragraph should change the reader's model and hand the next paragraph a live question or consequence.

Rewrite when any of these shapes dominate:

- a term appears as a two-word transition: “Interpretation follows”;
- the narrator tells the reader a sentence matters instead of building the reason it matters;
- several consecutive paragraphs begin with parallel declarations or `not X, but Y` corrections;
- author names and theories arrive in a literature-review procession;
- a generic manager vignette has invented times, meetings, or details to simulate reality;
- each sentence could be rearranged without changing the argument;
- a paragraph ends as a slogan, and the next paragraph restates it in bold;
- the prose breaks the fourth wall to name the framework, recap the chapter, or advertise what comes next;
- caveats repeatedly interrupt a sourced scene even though they belong in a note;
- abstract nouns such as signal, provenance, judgment, authorisation, or feedback arrive faster than the reader can use them.

Do not repair these failures with synonyms. Rebuild the causal passage: what happened, why the obvious account fails, what distinction becomes necessary, and what that distinction changes.

## 3. Evidence-path comparison

A strong comparison names a shared organisational problem without pretending the cases are equivalent.

Use four sentences or their narrative equivalent:

1. Case A's record became unfit or incomplete in this specific way.
2. Case B's record became unfit or incomplete in a different specific way.
3. Both matter because of this shared decision problem.
4. The difference changes what must be checked or redesigned.

Ford's all-green review and the gentian-violet lesion images are the benchmark. Ford shows omission: a relevant problem had not entered the charts. Violet ink shows an acquired process trace: the underlying lesion was unchanged while the image gained a cue associated with the prediction. The common problem appears before AI analysis—the record has a history. The remedy differs: making a problem safe to surface is not the same work as detecting a process artefact.

Keep the AI consequence equally precise. AI can accelerate preparation, comparison, and anomaly detection. It cannot recover a fact absent from its inputs, decide whether an acquired trace is legitimate evidence merely because it predicts, or own the human trade-off. “Human in the loop” therefore expands into contributions, timing, information, authority, answerability, and challenge—not one person beside an approval button.

## 4. Case and analogy audit

Use the sustained case for depth and a bounded lateral example for sight. A lateral example earns space when its factual surprise teaches the mechanism more cleanly than another management meeting would.

Write its return explicitly:

- what the non-human, historical, scientific, or geopolitical case reveals;
- where the equivalence ends;
- what humans or organisations do differently;
- how the comparison changes the decision now in view.

“Bees do not negotiate rights” states a boundary but does not complete the return. The return shows what humans use in place of the swarm's dance, quorum, or stop signal and why explanation, responsibility, and appeal alter the design problem.

## 5. Decision-Closure Contract

Choose the exercise entry deliberately.

### Sustained-case entry

Use the chapter's main case when the learner can act without rereading a long fact pattern and the same decision remains open. This protects continuity.

### Transfer-case entry

Use a common transfer case when everyone needs comparable choices, a reveal changes the meaning of the evidence, or the main case carries too much domain baggage. Introduce only the facts needed for the first commitment. After the reveal and revision, transfer the method to one decision the learner already owns.

A transfer case is not permission to start a second chapter. It uses the same mechanism and is linked by the chapter-to-exercise hinge. A case drawn from public records states its source boundary; a composite is labelled before the learner is asked to reason from it.

### One decision at a time

The participant should never face a wall of five open accordions or a dense form that asks for a complete policy before the first trade-off is visible. Each step makes one consequential move and leaves a trace for comparison.

A robust sequence is:

1. inspect the bounded starting material;
2. make one decision or allocation;
3. reveal a fact, provenance issue, counter-signal, or consequence;
4. revise, retain, or reject the first commitment with a reason;
5. make the decision executable by naming actor, action, boundary, and time;
6. state what evidence would continue, change, stop, or appeal it;
7. compare with the filled edition;
8. transfer the changed model to the learner's own context.

Do not repeat authorisation, action, and feedback under several labels. Every step must alter the artifact or expose a new trade-off.

### Enforced commitment

Copy that says “finish before revealing” is not a gate. The interface requires meaningful input in the named fields before enabling the reveal. Keep the participant's answer visible during comparison. Preserve keyboard access, focus movement, return, and mobile reading order.

AI enters only after the first commitment. Its prompt asks questions, surfaces an alternative, or stress-tests the evidence. It does not draft the initial answer, assign authority to itself, certify fairness, or decide for the participant.

## 6. Filled-edition parity

The blank artifact and completed edition use the same field IDs, labels, order, and case. A learner should be able to compare row by row without holding another screen in memory.

The filled edition is complete when it includes:

- named actors rather than “someone” or “the business”;
- a live alternative interpretation or course of action;
- evidence capable of discriminating between alternatives;
- a bounded authority and any consultation or challenge route;
- an executable action with actor and timing;
- a measurable review or reversal condition with owner and date;
- explicit fact, inference, composite, or reconstruction boundaries where needed.

It is one defensible answer, not the answer key. A field that does not apply says why. Placeholder advice such as “name the owner,” “review later,” or “a good design would” is not a filled edition.

## 7. Game feel without interface theatre

Use a scored or stateful game only when the learner's choice produces a visible consequence that changes what can be chosen next. The meaningful loop is commitment → consequence → explanation → revision → transfer.

Scores may represent the quality or cost of consequences. They must not reward guessing the teacher's vocabulary. Buttons, badges, confetti, progress bars, and card stacks do not create play by themselves. If the decision state does not change, retain the simpler workbook.

## 8. Experienced-Artifact Proof

Do not accept declared word counts, visual counts, return counts, or hidden states from the content model as final evidence. Derive them from the rendered artifact.

Use five independent reads:

1. **Narrative read:** Book only, straight through, at body-text size.
2. **Source-boundary read:** every factual clause, dialogue status, comparison, and reconstruction against its ledger.
3. **Visual read:** desktop and mobile geometry, chronology, caption, count, and picture-beats-words job.
4. **Exercise-journey read:** first commitment through reveal, revision, filled comparison, and transfer with keyboard and touch.
5. **Parity read:** same mechanism and decision across Book, Teaching, Slides, and exercise without identical density.

For a substantial build, assign these to separate read-only reviewers when agents are available. The orchestrator resolves conflicts and verifies the actual files. A reviewer summary is evidence about judgment, not proof that the artifact ran.
