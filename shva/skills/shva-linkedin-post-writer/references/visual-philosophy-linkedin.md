## Promoted: The Winning Composition Template (validated 2026-04-25)

For India-flavored posts (only when the optional India layer is invoked — typically Authority Redesign or Mental-Model Reframe themes with dharmic / Indic content), the winning visual idiom — landed after 10 iterations on the "Vivek Economy" post — is:

**Single Indian Protagonist + Indian-Art Geometric Environment**

Mirrors the atom-creator Microsoft / Nadella partnership-crisis composition, but with Indian visual DNA instead of Microsoft Metro tiles:

```
SUBJECT (LOWER-CENTER):
  Solo modern young Indian figure in profile silhouette, deep indigo
  Wearing a fitted graphic tee with a SINGLE recognizable mark on the
  chest (e.g., AI-sparkle starburst icon for AI-context posts, or a
  Pahari motif for cultural-purity posts) printed in incandescent
  white-gold
  Dark jeans, casual modern look
  Gaze directed up and slightly toward the brightest element

ENVIRONMENT (UPPER TWO-THIRDS):
  Vast halo of luminous saffron-gold Indian sacred geometry —
  yantras (Sri Yantra and others), mandala patterns, chakra wheels,
  kolam dot-patterns — arranged in an asymmetric organic constellation

FOCAL POINT:
  ONE central yantra (typically Sri Yantra) glowing brightest in
  incandescent white-gold with a soft halo, casting dramatic warm
  side-light onto the figure's profile

BACKGROUND:
  Saturated mythic vermilion-saffron field with radiating sun-ray
  gradients

MOOD:
  Cinematic, scroll-stopping, contemplative-but-charged
```

**Why this works (validated):**
1. Single figure = iconic anchor (like Nadella alone among Live Tiles)
2. Indian sacred geometry = AI rendered through cultural continuity (not robots, not circuits)
3. AI-sparkle-icon-on-tee = direct text-visual link (modern AI user identity legible)
4. Pop palette (vermilion + saffron-gold + indigo + amber) = bookshelf-loud
5. Asymmetric organic constellation = unique geometric signature (Principle 5 spatial memory)

**When to use this template:**
- ✅ India-flavored posts about AI / future of work (only when the optional India layer is invoked — never the default for systems-thinking posts)
- ✅ Posts with scriptural anchors (Katha Upanishad, Bhagavad Gita references)
- ❌ NOT for Constraint Migration posts with numerical lead (numbers carry the weight; image dilutes)
- ❌ NOT for posts that already have a strong industry-quote credential (would compete with the quote)

**Reference prompt template (SeedDream-concise, validated):**

> Flat editorial illustration with risograph grain, Indian miniature painting × Saul Bass simplicity. A solitary modern young Indian figure stands in the lower-center, profile silhouette in deep indigo, wearing a fitted graphic tee with [a luminous AI-assistant sparkle icon (6-pointed starburst, like the Gemini/Claude AI mark) | a Pahari [motif]] printed in white-gold on the chest, dark jeans, gaze directed up-right. Around the figure floats a vast space of luminous saffron-gold Indian sacred geometry — yantras, mandala patterns, chakra wheels, kolam dot-patterns — in an asymmetric organic constellation. One central Sri Yantra glows brightest in white-gold, casting dramatic warm side-light onto the figure's profile. Background: saturated mythic vermilion-saffron field with radiating sun-ray gradients. Cinematic atmosphere. Full-bleed to all four edges, no cream border, no white frame.
>
> 扁平插画, Riso印刷, 电影感, 戏剧性侧光. No human face, no extended fingers, no text, no logos, no machinery, no robots, no circuits, no blue digital aesthetics.

**Compositions to AVOID** (validated wrong directions from this iteration):
- ❌ Two figures back-to-back across time (temporal contrast — feels cliché)
- ❌ Pure abstract symbolism without a recognizable figure (disconnects from text)
- ❌ Multi-armed devotional deity (too religious, narrows the audience)
- ❌ Microsoft-Metro-tile copy with Indian rishi (literal copy of atom-creator course aesthetic)
- ❌ Two-figure narrative scenes (Nachiketa + Yama with offerings) — too theatrical for a single-image LinkedIn slot

## When to Generate (Visual Need Decision Gate)

Not every post needs a visual. Run this gate FIRST.

**Generate a visual when ≥1 condition fires:**
- Theme is **Release / Announcement** (visual recall on the framework/tool name compounds)
- Theme is **Future Scenario** (an evocative image makes abstract futures tangible)
- Theme is **Constraint Migration** or **Authority Redesign** AND there's a quotable CEO/operator line that can become visual (microphone/megaphone/courtroom metaphor)
- Theme is **Adoption Gap** (or Sequence Inversion with sharp before/after) AND the contrast can be rendered as a clean two-state image (mask, divided composition, before/after)
- Post anchors on a specific company OR a specific famous founder/leader (Type 1 or Type 2 viable)
- The Violation line is concrete enough to translate into a single dramatic moment

**Skip the visual when ≥1 condition fires:**
- Theme is **Constraint Migration with numerical lead** with the violation being a numerical comparison ($X vs $Y) — the numbers are the visual; an illustration dilutes them
- Post is dense data + framework where adding an image fragments attention
- The Violation is conceptual/abstract enough that no concrete moment can render it without becoming an infographic
- Post is text-heavy contrarian opinion that earns its weight through prose alone
- Generating an image would force a metaphor that doesn't connect to domain (VD3 fails)

**Borderline rule:** If genuinely unsure, skip. A great post without an image beats a great post with a mid image. The image must EARN its place.

**God-mode default:** When in god mode, skip the user gate and apply the heuristic above. Log the decision + reason for the learning loop.

## The Three Visual Types

**Priority order:** Company-Driven > Personality-Driven > Lateral (default).

### Type 1: Company-Driven

**When to use:** The post is centrally about ONE specific company AND that company has a design language recognizable from abstract elements alone — no logo, no wordmark, just colors, layouts, UI patterns.

**How it works:** Borrow the company's design DNA, not their products. Colors, grid structures, card elevations, UI surface patterns rendered as flat geometric abstraction. The viewer should feel "this is about X" within 2 seconds without any text.

**Near-logo treatment rules** (legally defensible, instantly recognizable):
- Modify ONE structural element of the company's iconic mark while preserving overall gestalt.
- Snapchat ghost: shift eye position. Apple: change bite to opposite side, square the leaf. Twitter/X bird: simplify to 3 strokes.
- The test: "A user knows what company this references, but a lawyer cannot claim trademark infringement."
- **NEVER** reproduce exact logos, wordmarks, registered slogans, or trademarked character designs.

**Brand DNA exemplars (LinkedIn-relevant):**
- **OpenAI** — soft-cool grays, blossom-like 8-petal motif, restrained sans, ample negative space. The hush is the brand.
- **Anthropic** — terra-cotta + ivory + black ink, hand-drawn-feeling shapes, asymmetric warmth. The texture is the brand.
- **Google** — 4-color system (#4285F4, #EA4335, #FBBC05, #34A853), card elevation with colored shadows, 8dp grid. Three colored cards at different elevations on white = instant Google.
- **Apple** — white space as luxury signal, single-object centered composition, precise soft shadow, rounded rectangle as recurring form. The ABSENCE of noise IS Apple.
- **Bloomberg** — amber on black grid. Any amber-on-black data layout reads as Bloomberg instantly.
- **Indian brands** — Zomato (red #E23744 + delivery grid), Swiggy (orange #FC8019 + route-line motif), CRED (black/white minimal luxury), Tata (blue/silver T-shape), IIM (deep navy + ochre, institutional gravitas).

**Pop-art legal defensibility:** Andy Warhol's Campbell's Soup Cans proved commercial design language IS legitimate artistic material when recontextualized. Trade dress protects "total look and feel," but educational illustration about a company using that company's design language is the most defensible use case.

### Type 2: Personality-Driven

**When to use:** A famous person anchors the post AND that person is recognizable by silhouette alone. Recognition test: show the silhouette to someone unfamiliar with the post. Can they guess who it is?

**Silhouette recognition hierarchy (5 tiers, strongest to weakest):**
1. **Props** (Tier 1): Gandhi's walking stick. Jobs' keynote remote. Churchill's cigar. Strongest signal — unique geometric shapes attached to the figure.
2. **Clothing shape** (Tier 2): Gandhi's dhoti, Jobs' turtleneck + jeans, Nehru's jacket collar.
3. **Exaggerated body proportions** (Tier 3): Bachchan's height, Tendulkar's compact stance, Tagore's beard width.
4. **Unique pose** (Tier 4): Tendulkar's low backlift, Bolt's lightning bolt celebration.
5. **Accessory position** (Tier 5): Gandhi's round spectacles, Nehru's boutonniere.

**Best silhouettes combine Tier 1 + Tier 2** (props + clothing). Walking stick + dhoti = Gandhi at any resolution.

**LinkedIn use case:** Posts about a specific founder's decision, a regulator's stance, a CEO's announcement. The silhouette becomes the visual subject.

**iPod silhouette campaign methodology** (TBWA\Chiat\Day, 2003-2008): construct silhouettes geometrically, do not trace photographs. Exaggerate limb length for dynamism. One non-silhouette element (the earbuds) anchors recognition.

### Type 3: Lateral (DEFAULT)

**When to use:** Everything that is not Company-Driven or Personality-Driven. The majority of posts.

**The lateral approach — refined.** "Lateral" does NOT mean "completely unrelated." The lateral leap should be in TREATMENT (how you render it), not in SUBJECT (what you render). Subject rhymes with domain. Treatment surprises.

**Every Lateral visual must demonstrably apply at least 3 of the 8 Lateral Thinking Principles below.**

## The 5-Step Lateral Thinking Process

For every Lateral image, run these 5 steps IN ORDER. Skipping to step 4 (treatment/metaphor) without doing 1-3 produces disconnected images.

1. **Start with the DOMAIN.** What is the actual world this post lives in? AI policy → regulatory courtrooms. Founder readiness → war rooms. Placement theater → graduation halls. Compensation arbitrage → trading floors. **Don't jump to metaphors yet.**

2. **Find the CHARACTERS that inhabit that domain.** Who are the people in this world? Regulator → judges, policy aides, lobbyists. War room → operators, dispatchers. Placement → graduates, recruiters, banners. These become silhouette figures.

3. **Find the VISUAL LANGUAGE of that domain.** What does this world LOOK like physically? Regulators → benches, gavels, marble columns, scrolls. War rooms → ticker walls, dashboards, switchboards, maps. This vocabulary becomes the environment.

4. **Pick a TREATMENT that creates tension with the domain.** This is the lateral leap — not in subject, but in HOW. Regulators rendered as Soviet-constructivist propaganda posters = unexpected (gravitas meets agitprop). Placement halls rendered as 1950s wedding photography = unexpected (mass-graduation-as-arranged-marriage commentary). Treatment should make a viewer say "I've never seen [domain] look like THIS."

5. **Tell the story through what HAPPENS TO the characters.** The single image still implies a narrative arc — captured at the moment of MAXIMUM tension. Confident → stumbling → collapsed → knowing. Pick the moment. Define the character's posture before writing the prompt.

**The one-sentence pitch test:** After completing all 5 steps, pitch the direction in one sentence. "A regulator's empty bench lit by amber dusk, rendered as Soviet constructivist poster." If a colleague says "oh cool" → proceed. If they say "what do you mean?" → iterate from Step 1.

**The failure pattern:** Jumping straight to Step 4 without doing 1-3. Produces: kathputli puppets for an Apple post (no domain connection), abstract geometry for anything (no characters). Anchor the world before elevating it.

## The 8 Lateral Thinking Principles

Apply at least 3 to every Lateral visual.

### Principle 1: Cognitive Dissonance as Hook

The visual subject bears no obvious surface relationship to the post topic. The gap forces the brain to build a bridge — and that bridge is the memorable association.

**The test:** Describe the image to someone who hasn't read the post. They should NOT guess the topic. Reveal the post topic. They should immediately understand the connection.

**Exemplars:** Cadbury Gorilla (gorilla playing drums sells chocolate via pure joy). Silk Cut (sliced purple silk sells cigarettes via the brand-name visual pun). Fevicol Egg (hen lays unbreakable egg sells glue via metaphor).

**Anti-pattern:** Post about AI regulation shows a gavel + AI chip icon. Subject IS the topic. No bridge, no memory.

### Principle 2: Character-as-Concept

The core concept becomes a physical CHARACTER — a being with form, posture, behavior. The character cannot be separated from the concept because the character IS the concept made flesh.

**The test:** Remove the character. Does the concept disappear with it?

**Exemplars:** Pixar's Inside Out (Joy IS golden upward light, Anger IS combustion). Dementors (depression as hooded floating cold). Amul Girl (Indian appetite embodied, 58 years).

**LinkedIn application:** A post about ambient AI agents could feature a "watcher" silhouette who appears at thresholds — the agent as a recurring character archetype. A post about Adoption Gap could feature a two-faced building (one face polished demo, one face frozen production).

### Principle 3: Visual Tradition Fusion

Combine exactly TWO disparate visual traditions — one provides STRUCTURE, one provides TREATMENT. One is pastiche; three is noise.

**The test:** Name the two traditions. Remove either. Does the image collapse into something ordinary?

**Exemplars:** Hipgnosis (surrealism + commercial album packaging). Wong Kar-wai (Hollywood Technicolor + European modernist time fragmentation). Neville Brody (Swiss grid + punk ethics).

**LinkedIn fusion candidates:**
- Indian miniature painting structure + Saul Bass treatment (mythic gravitas + film-poster simplicity)
- Soviet constructivist structure + Persian miniature treatment (propaganda angle + ornamental warmth)
- Saul Bass title-sequence structure + risograph print treatment (mid-century elegance + DIY texture)
- Bauhaus geometric structure + Ukiyo-e linework treatment (rigorous + flowing)
- 1950s wedding photography structure + Wes Anderson palette treatment (formal + saturated)

### Principle 4: Subject-Treatment Independence

Subject (WHAT appears) and treatment (HOW it's rendered) are independent axes. A pedestrian subject with extraordinary treatment beats an extraordinary subject with standard treatment. Never let both axes be standard.

**Exemplars:** Edward Weston's Pepper No. 30 (a pepper, but raked light makes it sculptural). William Eggleston's parking lots in dye-transfer saturation. Juergen Teller shooting Kate Moss with a cheap flash in a kitchen.

**LinkedIn application:** A post about LinkedIn Top Voice gaming could show a generic conference badge — but rendered as Edward Weston food still life. The mundane subject elevates through treatment.

### Principle 5: Spatial Memory Architecture

The image creates a unique GEOMETRIC PATTERN a viewer can sketch from memory 3 days later. Pattern is structural — in the arrangement of shapes, not the details of rendering.

**The test:** Three days later, can the viewer draw the basic geometric structure?

**Exemplars:** Vignelli's NYC Subway map (3 angles, geographically wrong, memorable forever). Otl Aicher's Munich Olympics pictograms (unique spatial signature per sport). Saul Bass Vertigo poster (one spiral = one memory).

**LinkedIn rule:** The image's geometric skeleton must be sketchable in 3 seconds. If the viewer can only describe it verbally ("some shapes on a dark background"), it fails.

### Principle 6: Constraint-Based Palette

Maximum 5-6 colors per image. Each with SEMANTIC meaning tied to the narrative. Constraint is meaning-making, not aesthetic preference.

**The test:** Can you write a color legend? Background = [meaning]. Accent = [meaning]. Failure = [meaning]. If a color has no assigned meaning, kill it.

**Exemplars:** El Lissitzky's "Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge" (Red = Bolsheviks, White = old guard, Black = battlefield). David Fincher's Se7en (red reserved for blood; everything else desaturated). Barbara Kruger (red bars + black Futura + white text — three elements, zero flexibility).

**LinkedIn-specific 5-color minimum (SeedDream-ready):**
- **Warm accent** (amber/coral/gold) — glow, hope, opportunity, the moment of insight
- **Cool accent** (navy/indigo/teal) — structure, authority, institution
- **Neutral** (sand/charcoal/warm gray) — background and secondary
- **Protagonist** (white or off-white silhouette color)
- **Signal** (red/coral, sparingly) — tension, failure, alarm

**Banned palettes** (for LinkedIn distinctness):
- Pure white #FFFFFF and any color where R, G, B all > #E0 (LinkedIn feed background blends in — your image disappears)
- Generic AI purple/blue gradients (looks like every Midjourney post since 2022)
- Pure black #000000 (use tinted darks like #0a0a0a or #1a1a2e)

### Principle 7: Emotional Arc Mirroring (single-image variant)

For a single image, the "arc" is captured at ONE moment — but the image's density, saturation, and compositional energy must match the post's emotional center of gravity.

**Map the post first, then design the image:**
- Post is **Constraint Migration with numerical lead** (numbers violation) → image should be CALM, structured, Bauhaus-grid energy. Density LOW. The numbers carry tension; image holds steady.
- Post is **Adoption Gap** → image should be DENSE, tense, late-Fincher energy. Saturation HIGH. Image performs the discomfort of pilots-succeeded-but-transformations-stalled.
- Post is **Future Scenario** → image should be QUIET, expectant, dawn-light energy. Saturation MID. Image holds promise.
- Post is **Release/Announcement** → image should be CONFIDENT, centered, Apple-like clarity. Density LOW. Image performs the ship moment.
- Post is **Constraint Migration** or **Authority Redesign** with CEO/operator quote → image should be COURTROOM-FORMAL, the quote-as-statement vibe. Density MID.
- Post is **Mental-Model Reframe** (fusion / two-domain weave) → image should show two domains weaving — bridge, braid, intersection. Density MID-HIGH.

### Principle 8: The Big Short Test

Would this visual work in a movie scene explaining the concept to a non-expert audience?

**Exemplars:** The Big Short used Margot Robbie in a bathtub for mortgage-backed securities, Jenga blocks for CDOs. Jonathan Jarvis used a waterfall for securitization tranches. Hans Rosling used animated bubbles for global income.

**Test:** Imagine a film director needs to explain the post's core concept in one visual. Would they use your image? If the metaphor requires a paragraph of explanation, it's too abstract. The viewer should FEEL the concept before reading the post.

## Theme x Visual Type Affinity (LinkedIn-specific)

| Theme (from rotation) | Primary Type | Secondary | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Constraint Migration with numerical lead** | Lateral | — | Almost always Lateral. Numbers violation needs calm Bauhaus rendering or pure data-as-art (Eggleston dye-transfer). |
| **Mental-Model Reframe** (fusion / two-domain weave) | Lateral | — | Two-domain weaving. Bridge/braid/intersection metaphors. Visual Tradition Fusion principle applies natively. |
| **Release / Announcement** | Company-Driven (if shipping for a known brand) | Lateral | If Rehearsal AI / Shiva-as-founder posts a tool: borrow institutional design DNA. Otherwise Lateral. |

| **Adoption Gap** | Lateral | Personality-Driven (if leader has a face) | Two-state/divided/mask compositions (demo room vs production floor). Strong on Cognitive Dissonance principle. |
| **Future Scenario** | Lateral | — | Threshold/horizon/dawn metaphors. Quiet expectancy. |

Primary Type is the default recommendation. Secondary applies when the specific post signals a famous person or dominant company.

## Classification Algorithm (run after post is drafted)

1. **Company dominance check:** Does the post center on ONE specific company AND does that company have a recognizable design language? If yes → **Type 1 Company-Driven**.
2. **Personality anchor check:** Does the post quote or center on ONE famous person AND would a viewer recognize their silhouette (≥2 of the 5 tiers)? If yes → **Type 2 Personality-Driven**.
3. **Default → Type 3 Lateral.** Apply ≥3 of the 8 principles.

## Output: The Visual Style Spec

After classification + lateral-thinking process, write a single short spec:

```
TYPE: [Company-Driven / Personality-Driven / Lateral]
KEY MOMENT: [the one sentence from the post that the image renders]
SUBJECT: [what appears in the frame — the character/object/scene]
TREATMENT: [the rendering language — fusion of two traditions]
PALETTE: [5-color list with semantic meaning per role]
COMPOSITION: [centered / rule-of-thirds / diagonal / split / asymmetric]
EMOTIONAL TEMPERATURE: [calm / tense / triumphant / quiet / dense]
ASPECT RATIO: [1.91:1 link-preview / 1:1 square / 4:5 portrait]
PRINCIPLES APPLIED: [list at least 3 of the 8]
ONE-SENTENCE PITCH: [the VD6 pitch test sentence]
```

This spec is the input to the SeedDream prompt construction step (see `seedream-prompt-rules.md`).

## LinkedIn-Specific Aspect Ratios

| Use case | Ratio | Pixel target | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Link-preview style** (single image attached to post with URL) | 1.91:1 | 1200×627 | LinkedIn's standard OG card. Renders cleanly in feed and link previews. |
| **Standalone square** (no URL, just image) | 1:1 | 1200×1200 | Best for posts where the image IS the hero, not supporting content. |
| **Vertical / portrait** (mobile-first) | 4:5 | 1200×1500 | Best when post is read primarily on mobile and image needs vertical space. Most LinkedIn engagement is mobile (88%) so vertical is often best. |

**Default for god mode:** **1:1 square (1200×1200)**. Most versatile for mobile + desktop. Override only if explicit signal.

After EVERY image generation, verify aspect ratio with `sips -g pixelWidth -g pixelHeight {file}`. SeedDream and Gemini both occasionally ignore ratio instructions.

## Visual Style Bans (universal)

- ❌ Visible faces (always — too much variation, breaks consistency, often glitches in SeedDream)
- ❌ Text in image (LinkedIn text overlays are user-rendered HTML, not baked into the image)
- ❌ Logos or wordmarks (legal + cliché)
- ❌ White-passing backgrounds (R, G, B all > #E0 — disappears against LinkedIn's white feed)
- ❌ Generic AI gradient backgrounds (purple-to-blue, the Midjourney 2022 default)
- ❌ Stock-photo energy (corporate handshake, woman-laughing-at-salad, suit-pointing-at-graph)
- ❌ Hands and fingers in close detail (SeedDream produces glitched hands consistently — keep them at sides, in pockets, or out of frame)
- ❌ Numeric data baked into the image (dates, percentages, dollar amounts — use uppercase taxonomic labels at most, max 2-4 per image)
- ❌ Decorative emoji-style icons (kills the editorial register)

## Reference: post → key-moment extraction

The "key moment" is the single sentence in the post the image renders. Extract it BEFORE classifying type. Atom-creator's VD8 (story-moment prompting) applies natively here.

**Extraction rule:** Find the sentence in the post that:
1. Carries the most concrete imagery (a noun or verb that points to a tangible scene)
2. Sits at the post's emotional peak (often the violation line or the final challenge)
3. Would survive being made into a movie still

If no sentence in the post passes all three, the post may be too abstract for a visual — return to the Decision Gate and consider skipping the image.

Examples:
- Post about ambient AI: key moment = "your fitness tracker spots a stress spike thirty minutes before your final round" → image renders the wrist + the unseen interview room behind a door
- Post about Adoption Gap (e.g., "Pilots succeed but transformations fail"): key moment = the disconnect between polished demo room and frozen production floor → image renders a polished glass-walled demo space with a frozen production floor visible through a doorway
- Post about Constraint Migration with numerical lead: key moment = "$500 of API calls did what a $6,000 SaaS subscription couldn't" → image renders two stacks (or skip image — numerical-lead Constraint Migration posts often don't need one)
