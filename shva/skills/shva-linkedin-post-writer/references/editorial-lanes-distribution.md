# Editorial Lanes, Distribution, and Learning Ledger

> **What this covers:** The dual-candidate contract, candidate-selection gate, one-record-per-topic ledger, genre-specific visual routes, cold-start distribution rhythm, and outcome review.
>
> **When to load:** After the evidence-backed thesis exists and before outlining serious enterprise-AI or AI/work/education posts. Load it again for visual selection, wiki save, and post-publication learning.

## Governing Idea

Reach and authorship are not opposites. They are two editorial treatments of the same earned idea.

For every serious enterprise-AI or AI/work/education topic, develop:

1. **Attention candidate** - the version most likely to earn immediate relevance from the intended audience. It leads with a recognisable stake, contradiction, consequence, or high-signal number; reaches the useful reframe quickly; and remains credible to a senior reader.
2. **Authorship candidate** - the version closest to Shiva's durable taste. It privileges connected cadence, ordinary-work texture, category correction, sequence/constraint/authority thinking, and an earned systems reframe.

These are candidates, not separate content identities. Personal-taste posts are normally authorship-first. `State of Humans` is normally comic-first. Use the dual-lane comparison there only when it improves a caption or selection decision; do not force two prose posts around every idea.

## Shared-Thesis Invariant

Both candidates must preserve the same:

- evidence-backed central claim;
- material facts, numbers, source confidence, and caveats;
- target decision-maker and intended change in thought;
- operational example or causal mechanism;
- public/private provenance boundary.

The attention candidate may compress, reorder, or sharpen the reveal. It may not exaggerate certainty, manufacture conflict, introduce a different thesis, use fear as a substitute for stakes, or drift into a generic creator voice.

If the two candidates cannot be compared without changing the thesis, the topic is not ready. Return to research or create two separate topic records.

## Candidate Construction Contract

| Dimension | Attention candidate | Authorship candidate |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | Recognisable consequence, contradiction, question, or source-backed number | Specific observation, category correction, or ordinary work scene |
| Pace | Faster route from hook to consequence | More room for connected reasoning and conceptual earning |
| Texture | One vivid operational example; no ornamental detail | Concrete workflow texture: approvals, exceptions, chasing, Excel, WhatsApp, rework, judgment |
| Reframe | Clear and usable before reader fatigue | Distinctive systems reframe that compounds Shiva's body of work |
| Close | Memorable consequence, principle, or decision question | Earned line that changes the category, without sermon or engagement bait |
| Failure mode | Sensationalism, staccato ladders, broad anxiety, generic virality | Density, delayed relevance, abstraction, writing for people already inside the argument |

Both candidates must pass the canonical Hook Test, SUCCESs check, Cringe Test, voice/variability check, source gate, and mechanics gate.

## Selection Gate

Score each candidate from 0-2 on every row. Do not recommend a candidate with any zero.

| Test | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intended-audience recognition | Stakes are obscure | Stakes become clear in the body | The right reader recognises the problem before `See more` |
| Evidence integrity | Claim outruns evidence | Accurate but loosely mapped | Every material claim is traceable and appropriately qualified |
| Shiva distinctiveness | Generic creator voice | Some Shiva texture | Reframe could belong only to Shiva's body of work |
| Executive utility | Observation only | Useful interpretation | Changes a decision, diagnostic, or conversation |
| Readability | Dense, abrupt, or confusing | Clear with friction | Connected, natural, and easy for an emerging-tech novice |
| Portfolio contribution | Repeats a recent post | Adjacent but defensible | Advances a different lens, structure, or genre role |

Then write a recommendation in one sentence:

`Recommend {attention|authorship} because {specific audience and portfolio reason}; preserve {alternate} because {what it protects or can teach us}.`

The higher score is not automatically selected. Prefer the candidate that best serves the slot's job:

- executive and education slots: attention may win when it improves entry without weakening the thesis;
- personal-taste slot: authorship normally wins unless its relevance is too delayed;
- comic slot: standalone visual comprehension and the closing principle govern selection.

## Qualified Outcome Hierarchy

The objective is not maximum undifferentiated reach. Evaluate in this order:

1. training/advisory inquiries and relevant invitations;
2. profile viewers, site visits, and relevant new followers;
3. substantive comments, saves, and sends from senior enterprise, transformation, HR/L&D, GCC, university, or policy audiences;
4. audience-demographic fit and out-of-network reach;
5. impressions and reactions as context.

LinkedIn labels impressions and members reached as estimates. Do not call impressions an open rate, and do not treat a large impression count as proof of authority or commercial intent.

## Twelve-Post Review Rule

Freeze the four genre slots until **12 posts have been published and publication-verified** under this benchmark cycle.

At review:

- compare attention vs authorship selections overall and within genre where sample size permits;
- compare qualified outcomes against each post's pre-publication prediction;
- inspect audience titles, seniority, sectors, substantive comment quality, profile movement, site visits, inquiries, and invitations;
- separate copy, topic, visual, timing, and external-event explanations;
- treat impressions as reach context, not the objective;
- do not infer causality from one post or move a slot because of one outlier.

Recommend a schedule or lane change only when a repeated pattern is visible. Record the evidence, competing explanation, confidence, and proposed next 12-post test.

## Visual Contribution Gate

A visual must do at least one job the prose cannot do as efficiently:

- arrest attention with a credible contradiction;
- expose a mechanism, transition, or decision surface;
- make a reusable mental model immediately legible;
- establish `State of Humans` series recognition.

Reject the attachment when it merely summarises the post, paginates prose, repeats a number already functioning as the hook, needs extensive caption explanation, or becomes decorative corporate content.

### Executive enterprise AI

**Prefer:** a premium editorial evidence surface or restrained data-art that a senior leader could take into a meeting. Show one contradiction and one decision question. Use evidence, operational objects, negative space, and a restrained palette.

**Reject:** deck-like frameworks, consulting quadrants, dashboards without a decision, stock executives, glowing brains, robots, blue-purple AI gradients, or text-heavy infographics.

### AI, work, and education

**Prefer:** a legible human/work transition or evidence surface that exposes how learning, apprenticeship, judgment, assessment, or work-readiness changes. The mechanism must remain understandable at feed size.

**Reject:** classroom stock photography, graduation-cap symbolism, generic future-of-work art, sentimental student imagery, or diagrams that restate the prose.

### Personal taste and wider systems thinking

**Prefer:** an art-led lateral image, surreal or absurdist editorial scene, unusual photographic treatment, or one visual contradiction. Usually use little or no text. Preserve intellectual seriousness and visual memorability.

**Reject:** explanatory infographic styling, motivational quote cards, literal illustrations, ornamental weirdness without a conceptual bridge, or imitation of a named living artist. Translate useful properties such as restraint, hierarchy, negative space, tension, or data density instead.

### `State of Humans`

**Prefer:** the established monochrome editorial-comic system, stable recurring cast, sparse panels, reality-based dark humour, and standalone comprehension. The post caption adds a memorable principle; it does not recap the joke.

**Reject:** colourful cartoon styling that weakens seriousness, inconsistent characters, unexplained visual events, staccato dialogue, a joke dependent on reading the caption first, or a branded lockup louder than the comic.

For every genre, no visual is a valid and often superior decision. Record the contribution or the reason for skipping.

## Research Rationale and Limits

- LinkedIn's next-generation feed architecture combines semantic relevance, member/content/network signals, recent behavioural sequences, and both active and passive feedback; its separate dwell-time work explains why a reader's pause can matter even without a reaction. This supports relevance, readability, and portfolio consistency, while ruling out deterministic "one trick" claims: [Engineering the next generation of LinkedIn's Feed](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/feed/engineering-the-next-generation-of-linkedins-feed) and [Leveraging dwell time to improve member experiences](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/feed/leveraging-dwell-time-to-improve-member-experiences-on-the-linkedin-feed).
- Buffer's 2026 analysis of 4.8 million LinkedIn posts found the strongest aggregate windows had shifted toward weekday late afternoons and evenings, with Wednesday 16:00 and Friday 15:00-16:00 among the leading slots and Wednesday-Friday generally stronger than Monday-Tuesday. This informed the Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday premium slots; Monday 18:00 is deliberately the lower-stakes authorship slot. The study is cross-account observational evidence, not a guarantee for Shiva's audience: [The Best Time to Post on LinkedIn in 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin/).
- LinkedIn's own post analytics expose impressions, members reached, saves, sends, profile viewers, followers gained, link visits, and audience demographics. This supports the qualified-outcome ledger, while LinkedIn explicitly notes that reach numbers are estimates: [Post analytics for your content](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a516971/post-analytics-for-your-content).
- LinkedIn and Edelman's 2024 research reports that senior decision-makers value credible research, novel insight, and an early reason to continue reading; it also connects high-quality thought leadership with later consideration. This supports evidence-led hooks and executive utility, not sensationalism or guaranteed conversion: [Reach Beyond The Ready](https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/research-and-insights/b2b-thought-leadership-research-impact-linkedin-edelman).
- LinkedIn's 2025 synthesis says decision-makers use thought leadership to assess capability, and highlights topical timeliness and actionability. This supports the attention candidate and serious portfolio rhythm, but does not establish a deterministic feed-ranking formula: [Why B2B Thought Leadership Matters](https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/content-marketing/the-importance-of-b2b-thought-leadership-content-and-how-to-get-it-right).

Platform behaviour, audience availability, and attribution can change. Treat the schedule and candidate predictions as hypotheses. Use the 12-post ledger to learn from Shiva's actual audience rather than repeating generic "algorithm" advice.

## Publication Boundary

Every unattended automation is **research-and-draft only**. It may create candidates, recommend one, prepare visuals, save the wiki record, and propose the benchmark time.

It must not save a LinkedIn draft, schedule, publish, react, comment, delete, upload, or otherwise perform a LinkedIn write action without Shiva's explicit later approval for the exact artifact and time. Approval of the strategy, skill, automation, or schedule pattern is not approval of an individual post.

After explicit approval, follow the current LinkedIn publishing protocol and verify each state separately. A pending schedule is not publication proof; a publication receipt is not exact public-body proof.
