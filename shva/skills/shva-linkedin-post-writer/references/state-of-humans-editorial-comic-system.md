# `State of Humans` Editorial Comic System

> **What this covers:** the statement-under-the-statement analysis, causal joke
> architecture, recurring cast, silent GPT witness, restrained newspaper visual
> language, copy, caption, alt text, production checks, and publication metadata
> for Shiva Kakkar's `State of Humans` series.
>
> **When to load:** for every `State of Humans`, editorial-comic, comic-first, or
> reality-based institutional-satire request. Load it after the evidence-backed
> thesis exists and **before** panel scripting, a closing principle, an image
> prompt, a caption, or alt text.

## Precedence and Boundary

This is the controlling visual and editorial reference for `State of Humans`.
Its deliberate use of a robot witness and sparse embedded text supersedes the
generic no-robot and no-text defaults in `visual-philosophy-linkedin.md` and
`seedream-prompt-rules.md` for this genre only. The canonical LinkedIn writer
still governs evidence, source hierarchy, private provenance, factual restraint,
the Cringe Test, mechanics, wiki capture, approval, and publication boundaries.

Do not use this system to imitate a named living cartoonist. Translate the useful
properties Shiva selected — monochrome newspaper restraint, economy of line,
ordinary human settings, dry institutional irony, negative space, and a punchline
that survives without spectacle — into an original recurring visual language.

## Strategic Job

`State of Humans` is not a lighter version of a serious post. It is a distinct
editorial instrument: a compact piece of causal analysis that a reader can grasp
before reading the caption.

The series should:

- expose the gap between a displayed claim and an operative reality;
- show the ordinary mechanism that produces the gap;
- make institutional language collide with what the institution itself caused;
- remain credible enough that a practitioner recognises the workflow;
- leave a portable principle, not a coined name for the phenomenon;
- widen cultural reach without weakening Shiva's authority on AI adoption,
  strategy, work, education, and society.

The target reaction is not merely “funny.” It is: “I have seen exactly this, but I
had not noticed what the sequence was doing.”

## The Statement Under the Statement

Before writing a joke, investigate the public statement as a system. Complete
this worksheet in the private production record:

| Question | What to establish |
|---|---|
| **Displayed claim** | What does the institution publicly say it values, changed, launched, enabled, or measured? |
| **Audience for the display** | Who needs to believe it — buyers, boards, admissions prospects, accreditors, investors, employees, media? |
| **Costly action avoided** | What sincere investment, redesign, permission, capability, or accountability would make the claim real? |
| **Operative constraint** | What rule, budget, system, approval path, incentive, or technical choice prevents the desired behaviour? |
| **Compliant human attempt** | What ordinary action does a reasonable employee, student, manager, or faculty member take? |
| **Displaced blame** | Who or what is diagnosed when the system produces the predictable result? |
| **Official story that survives** | What dashboard, press release, brochure, metric, or meeting conclusion remains after the causes disappear? |
| **Fair alternative** | Could ordinary incompetence, risk, cost, or timing explain the same result? What evidence makes the sharper reading warranted? |

The analysis may uncover a hidden agenda, but it must not assume one merely
because the outcome is foolish. Prefer an observable incentive and causal chain
over mind-reading. If the hidden-agenda interpretation cannot survive the fair
alternative, narrow the claim or discard the comic.

### The display-versus-substance test

Ask two separate questions:

1. What costly proof would demonstrate that the announced change exists in
   practice?
2. What cheaper signal can create the appearance of that change?

The comic begins when the cheaper signal is rewarded while the costly proof is
avoided. Examples include a course title instead of changed assessment, a licence
announcement instead of usable access, a completion count instead of changed
work, or an adoption dashboard that deletes the restrictions that produced low
use.

## Scope Calibration

The user's seed is evidence, not a prison. Narrow or broaden it when the change
improves truth, credibility, comprehension, and wit together.

- **Narrow** when the seed contains several grievances. Choose the one ordinary
  workflow whose contradiction can be shown without explanation.
- **Broaden** when a highly specific example is only one instance of a more
  recognisable human mechanism and the generalisation remains warranted.
- **Stop** when broadening creates a claim about “all companies,” “all faculty,”
  or “all employees” that the evidence cannot support.
- **Preserve** concrete artefacts — a login screen, reimbursement form, access
  policy, course assessment, dashboard, brochure, or review meeting — because
  they carry causal proof inside the image.

## Story Architecture

### Default: the four-beat causal chain

Use a natural 2×2 reading order. Each panel has one job.

1. **Mandate** — a leader, system, or institution states the desired behaviour in
   ordinary language. Establish who asked for what. No irony is required yet.
2. **Constraint** — the person encounters the first concrete system barrier. Show
   the exact operational object: blocked access, missing licence, disabled tool,
   incompatible process, unavailable data, or policy error.
3. **Compliant attempt** — the same person follows the approved route and meets the
   second barrier: permission, procurement, reimbursement, assessment, approval,
   or escalation. This beat proves that the failure is not simple laziness.
4. **Institutional verdict** — the institution reports the result while erasing
   panels 2 and 3 from its explanation. The displaced diagnosis is the punchline.
   Keep this panel sparse.

The causal chain must be readable without the LinkedIn caption:

`mandate -> system blocks action -> human follows process -> system blames human`

If a reader cannot reconstruct that chain from the image alone, rewrite before
rendering. Do not use the caption to repair missing context.

### Single-panel alternative

Use one panel when a single scene can hold the claim, avoided action, and
contradiction simultaneously — for example, an AI-labelled course announcement
beside a robot holding an ignored “hire me” sign. The scene must still reveal the
statement under the statement; it cannot be a generic reaction cartoon.

During development, make both one-panel and four-panel treatments for every
episode unless Shiva explicitly waives one format. Select by standalone
comprehension, causal force, and feed-size legibility — not by the amount of work
already invested. Preserve the unselected treatment as private production
evidence; do not publish both by default.

## Joke Engineering

### Reality first

- Begin from a scene a practitioner could recognise, not from an abstract slogan.
- Let the contradiction emerge from ordinary policy, process, and managerial
  language. Do not make people cartoonishly evil or stupid.
- Keep dialogue grammatical and conversational. Cryptic fragments and dramatic
  one-word ladders create confusion, not wit.
- Establish context before any shocking or unusual visual event. Unexplained
  injury, blood, chaos, or a floating object reads as an image-generation error.
- A comic-first reader must understand who is doing what, what failed, and why the
  final verdict is unfair before opening the post.

### The hard landing

The joke lands when the final official statement is technically plausible but
causally dishonest. The last panel should make the earlier panels snap together,
not add a new subplot.

Prefer:

- dry administrative language against visibly absurd causes;
- a dashboard, brochure, press line, training count, policy label, or meeting
  verdict that reveals the contradiction;
- restrained faces and an impassive witness;
- a reader-completed inference rather than a character explaining the moral.

Reject:

- generic “AI is ironic” jokes;
- zany antics, slapstick, memes, or brightly expressive cartoon energy;
- a narrator explaining why the scene is hypocritical;
- a coined phenomenon such as “The Bypass Signal” or “The Adoption Paradox”;
- punchlines that depend on hostility toward a profession or institution;
- jokes that become true only after the caption supplies missing facts.

## The Closing Principle

The caption begins with one portable principle distilled from the comic. It is
not the series title, a phenomenon name, or a recap.

### Construction test

A strong principle:

- works beyond the exact scene while remaining earned by it;
- contains a clean contrast, reversal, or cause-versus-measure tension;
- is concise enough to remember after one reading;
- sounds like an observation about human systems, not a management slogan;
- does not tell the reader what to feel;
- does not overclaim intent when the comic only proves incentives or process.

Aim for one sentence and normally no more than 18 words. Brevity is not licence
for obscurity; connected grammar beats staccato fragments.

Validated examples:

- “Nothing travels faster than the future in an admissions brochure.”
- “A metric can be accurate about the result and dishonest about the reason.”

These examples are standards, not templates. Do not repeat their syntax every
week.

### Caption contract

The default LinkedIn caption is deliberately short:

```text
[One portable principle.]

State of Humans · NN

#[series/topic tags: 3-5 total]
```

Use `State of Humans · NN` exactly so recognition compounds. Hashtags describe
the actual professional territory: for example `#StateOfHumans`,
`#EnterpriseAI`, `#AIAdoption`, `#AIGovernance`, `#FutureOfWork`, or an
education-specific equivalent. Do not stuff synonyms for search.

Do not:

- narrate what happens in the panels;
- introduce characters the image should have established;
- add a long lesson that steals the punch;
- make Shiva's commercial ambition explicit;
- ask an engagement-bait question;
- call the episode a “truth bomb” in public copy.

## Series Cast Bible

### Humans

- Use human characters for ordinary organisational life. Within an episode, the
  same employee, manager, dean, faculty member, or executive must retain face,
  hair, clothing, proportions, and role across panels.
- Keep the cast globally neutral and non-region-coded unless geography is
  essential to the evidence. Do not make characters look Indian merely because
  Shiva writes from India.
- Expressions are restrained and scene-appropriate: concentration, mild concern,
  hesitation, or confusion. Avoid broad smiles in a failure panel and melodrama
  in an administrative scene.
- No one is a moustache-twirling villain. The system is visible through the
  sequence of ordinary actions.

### The silent GPT witness — HARD continuity lock

The robot is present in the frame but is not automatically a party to the scene.
It witnesses human confusion the way an impassive observer might watch a ritual
whose contradiction is obvious from outside.

Preserve the exact model across panels and episodes:

- rounded-rectangle head with the established proportions;
- one short antenna;
- stacked neck rings;
- cylindrical torso and articulated cylindrical joints;
- mouthless face;
- two small, solid black, normal dot eyes of identical size and spacing;
- monochrome ink rendering consistent with the human linework.

The eyes do **not** widen, become hollow, change shape, acquire pupils, glow, or
turn into an emoji expression. The robot remains emotionally neutral. Its gaze or
body orientation may change to map attention, but its geometry and emotional
register may not. Do not add a shocked reaction to make the punchline legible;
strengthen the causal sequence instead.

The witness can become an active participant only when the episode's mechanism
requires it. That choice must be explicit in the production brief.

## Visual Language

### Stable system

- Exact masthead: `STATE OF HUMANS`.
- Monochrome newspaper-editorial linework on warm off-white paper.
- Black/charcoal ink, sparse mid-grey wash, generous negative space.
- Restrained, globally neutral office, classroom, corridor, meeting-room, or
  public-setting details.
- Original editorial drawing language informed by economy, quiet absurdity, and
  observational cartooning; never direct imitation of a named living artist.
- Small, low-contrast `shivakakkar.com` near the lower-right edge. It is
  provenance, not a campaign lockup.
- No honorific, role line, topic label, centred signature, or “GENAI ADOPTION”
  footer.

### Red is causal syntax, not decoration

Reserve muted red for the smallest set of words or states that trace the failure
through the system. Everything else stays monochrome.

For example, if three labels carry the causal thread, only those labels may be
red: `ACCESS BLOCKED`, `UNAUTHORIZED`, `LOW`. Do not colour headings, borders,
icons, furniture, clothing, or the robot to “balance” the accent.

The viewer should be able to follow the red marks and recover the obstruction.
If red does not encode causality, remove it.

### Panel composition and text

- Default canvas: square 1200×1200 for a four-panel feed hero unless the exact
  approved artifact establishes another ratio.
- Use a clear 2×2 grid with consistent gutters and an unambiguous top-left ->
  top-right -> bottom-left -> bottom-right sequence.
- Give each panel one dominant idea and one focal object.
- Use one to three short text elements per panel. Prefer realistic interface,
  notice, board, or dialogue copy over narration.
- Keep exact failure-state wording large enough to survive feed reduction.
- Keep the last panel visually sparse: one verdict surface, the relevant humans,
  and the silent witness. Remove decorative laptops, loose files, extra screens,
  or props that compete with the diagnosis.
- Every hand, device, speech bubble, screen, and character must belong to a visible
  person or causal action. A disembodied hand, third phone, duplicate character,
  unexplained document, or “ghost” object is a production failure.
- Do not fill space with copy. Negative space supplies seriousness.

## Production Workflow

### 1. Write the causal brief

Record:

```markdown
Displayed claim:
Audience for the display:
Costly action avoided:
Operative constraint:
Compliant human attempt:
Displaced blame:
Official story:
Fair alternative checked:
Episode cast:
Robot role: witness | participant
Closing principle candidates:
```

Do not script until the causal brief is coherent.

### 2. Script before rendering

Write each panel as `scene / visible text / causal job`. Read only the four panel
descriptions, without the caption, to another reader or a fresh agent. If they
cannot state the causal chain, revise the script.

### 3. Lock continuity explicitly

Before generation or drawing, create a compact continuity block containing the
human cast descriptions, exact robot geometry, masthead, palette, grid, and
provenance placement. Repeat the block unchanged for every generated panel or
edit pass. When editing one panel, protect all approved elements elsewhere.

### 4. Generate the master

Save an editable source when the rendering method permits it, plus a lossless
master PNG. Do not schedule from an exploratory render, prompt preview, contact
sheet, or earlier revision.

### 5. Inspect at two sizes

Inspect the exact candidate:

1. full-size, for character continuity, object ownership, text accuracy, panel
   order, red semantics, line quality, and rendering artefacts;
2. feed-size, normally 600×600 for a square, for masthead recognition, panel
   order, essential copy legibility, robot continuity, and punchline visibility.

The image fails if only zoomed inspection makes the story understandable.

### 6. Create a transport-safe publish rendition when needed

If the cloud MCP or LinkedIn transport rejects the lossless master because of
payload size, create a visually equivalent high-quality JPEG from the approved
master. Preserve dimensions and composition. Do not regenerate the artwork.

Record both files and their hashes:

- **master**: editable/lossless source of truth;
- **publish rendition**: exact uploaded derivative, MIME type, dimensions, byte
  size, filename, and SHA-256.

Run the full-size and feed-size visual inspection again on the publish rendition.
Approval of the master does not automatically approve a visibly degraded or
otherwise different transport file.

### 7. Run the pre-scheduling usability gate

Before every scheduling action, load the `design-everyday-things` skill and run
its five-row Quick Diagnostic on the exact image-plus-caption artifact:

- discoverability;
- evaluation;
- recovery;
- mapping;
- constraints.

Require 2/2 on every row, total 10/10. Record all five rows in the approval ledger
and wiki audit. Revise and rerun the complete diagnostic if any row fails.

## Alt Text and Delivery Metadata

Alt text serves the person who cannot see the image and the system that needs an
accurate semantic description. It is not a hidden marketing pitch.

Write alt text that:

- begins with `State of Humans` and identifies the format, such as “four-panel
  editorial comic”;
- names the professional territory naturally — enterprise AI adoption,
  education, work, governance, or society — when the image supports it;
- describes the causal sequence in reading order;
- quotes only the few visible labels necessary to understand the failure;
- identifies the same silent, neutral robot as a recurring witness when relevant;
- remains factual and avoids interpreting unshown intent;
- does not mention a desire to rank, become a visionary, win training work, or
  manipulate search.

Use a stable, descriptive filename such as:

`state-of-humans-03-resistance-dashboard-publish-v04.jpg`

Before scheduling, read back and verify the exact:

- caption text;
- image hash;
- filename;
- MIME type;
- dimensions and byte size;
- authored alt text;
- scheduled UTC time and corresponding Asia/Kolkata time.

A saved cloud draft is not a schedule. A pending schedule is not publication. A
publication receipt is not exact public-body proof. Preserve those states
separately.

## Revision-Earned Failure Library

These are durable lessons from the series' development. Treat them as rejection
tests, not optional taste notes.

| Failure | Why it failed | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Named phenomenon (`The Bypass Signal`, generic “Trust Test”) | Sounds manufactured and weakens observational authority | Show the mechanism; leave one portable principle |
| Caption explains the panels | Removes shock and makes the image dependent on prose | Make the comic standalone; caption only the principle and series marker |
| Staccato or cryptic dialogue | Reader cannot reconstruct the event | Use connected, ordinary workplace language |
| Shock without context | Blood, chaos, or reaction becomes unexplained decoration | Establish the ordinary scene and causal trigger first, or remove the shock |
| Colourful comic styling | Makes the serious institutional argument feel juvenile | Use restrained monochrome newspaper-editorial treatment |
| Robot model or eye drift | Breaks the recurring witness and series memory | Reuse the exact geometry and normal dot eyes; change gaze only |
| Exaggerated robot reaction | Tells the reader how to feel and becomes visually inconsistent | Keep the witness impassive; strengthen the human-system contradiction |
| Smiling humans in the failure panel | Contradicts the consequence | Use restrained confusion, concern, or administrative neutrality |
| Extra hand, phone, file, laptop, or duplicate character | Reads as a generation glitch and breaks causal ownership | Remove every object without a visible owner and story job |
| Dense last panel | Buries the official verdict | Keep only the verdict surface, relevant people, and witness |
| Loud Shiva branding | Makes an editorial work look like a campaign asset | Use only discreet `shivakakkar.com` at lower right |
| Region-coded cast by default | Narrows a general human observation without analytical reason | Use globally neutral characters unless geography is evidence |

## Release Gate

A `State of Humans` artifact survives only when every answer is yes:

1. Can an image-first reader state the causal chain without the caption?
2. Does the joke reveal the statement under the statement rather than merely mock
   the displayed claim?
3. Is the inference grounded in observable actions, incentives, or constraints?
4. Does each panel advance mandate, constraint, compliant attempt, or verdict?
5. Are humans and the robot continuous, with normal neutral robot eyes?
6. Is every object owned and every red element causal?
7. Does the last panel land without explanation?
8. Is the closing principle memorable, grammatical, portable, and unbranded?
9. Does the caption preserve the punch rather than repeat it?
10. Did the exact production file pass full-size, feed-size, metadata, and 10/10
    usability checks?

If any answer is no, revise. Do not schedule around the failure.
