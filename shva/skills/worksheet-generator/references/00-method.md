# The Session Worksheet Method (the heart)

The repeatable way to turn a topic plus rough ideas into a worksheet that teaches by being filled in.
Design backward from the target shape, build the spine first in plain prose, then lay it out.

## The target shape

- **One spine, carried forward.** The whole sheet is one idea traced to its end. Each stage hands a
  concrete value to the next: a "carry forward" band at the bottom of one page reappears as a "from
  the previous page" band at the top of the next. The participant should feel the chain.
- **A stage is a page.** One self-contained move per page. Dense moves (a workflow to draw, a 2×2 to
  fill) may take two pages, split at a natural seam ("Stage N, continued").
- **AI-resistant by construction.** Every box demands a name, number, place, or consequence from the
  participant's own workplace. A generic answer is visibly wrong. State the house rule up front:
  *"if a sentence you write could have been written by someone who has never seen your workplace, it
  is the wrong answer."* The tool becomes the foil, and distrusting the generic answer IS the lesson.
- **Self-contained.** No facilitator narration required to fill it. Every field carries its own short
  "how to think" helper and a faint worked **Example:**. Thread ONE worked example through every stage
  so the sheet reads as a complete case.
- **A companion questioner GPT, not a content GPT.** The AI used in the room is a questioner that
  interrogates assumptions and pushes the participant to their own specifics. See
  `decision-decomposer-gpt.md` for the pattern to adapt per topic.

## The content arc (build this first, in prose)

1. **Open on a felt problem, reframed.** Start where the participant already hurts (a vacancy, a late
   report), then a questioning move reveals the real unit underneath (usually a *decision*, a
   *constraint*, or a *workflow*). The reframe is the hook.
2. **Decompose.** Cut the unit into its parts (e.g. prediction vs judgment). This is the analytical
   heart and supplies the axes that return later.
3. **Trace and choose.** Place the unit in its system (the workflow), surface the fork (incremental vs
   structural), make them commit.
4. **Land the consequence.** Show what moves once they act: the constraint jumps, power follows, the
   structure redraws. Reuse an earlier distinction as the axes of a final 2×2 so the payoff feels
   inevitable, not bolted on ("re-obvious at higher resolution").
5. **Capstone sentence.** A single fill-in sentence that forces them to state the whole chain in their
   own words.

**Worked-example discipline:** pick ONE concrete case and thread it through every Example line. It
makes the sheet self-teaching and shows the expected altitude of an answer.

Not every topic is a decision. The arc generalizes: *felt problem → name the real unit → decompose it
→ place it in its system and choose → show the consequence → state it in one sentence.* Swap the unit
(a negotiation, a metric, a habit, a risk) and keep the spine.

## Per-page structure

- **Stage header:** big number + plain descriptive title + one-line subtitle.
- **"How to use" strip at the top — mechanical only.** One or two sentences of *what to do*. Slim.
- **"Be Mindful!" placed in context, NOT stacked under How-to.** This is the *how to think* insight
  (an analogy or worked mini-example). Put it immediately before, or right after, the specific field
  where that thinking bites, so they chew on it at the moment of work. Stacking How-to + Be Mindful! at
  the top is the anti-pattern.
- **Each field = a real question + a one-line "how to think" helper (complete sentence, in brackets) +
  a writing box sized to the ask + a faint `Example:` under the box.**
- **Fill-in-the-blank sentences** for the reframe and the capstone, each with a faint worked example
  beneath.
- **Carry-forward / from-previous bands** connect the pages.
- **Signature block with real signing space** (blank area above the line, label below).
