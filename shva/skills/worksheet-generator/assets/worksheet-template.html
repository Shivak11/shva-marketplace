Find the Real Decision

The thing slowing you down is often a decision no one has made, not a task that is pending.

How to use

Open the Decision Decomposer GPT and tell it the pressure you feel at work. Let it keep asking until you reach the decision underneath. Fill the boxes as you talk.

1.  What is the pressure you would name first?
The symptom, in the words you would use with a colleague.
Example: We keep losing good people in the regional sales team and the numbers are slipping.
Be Mindful!

A task is something you do. A decision is a choice you make. “We are short of people” is a task. The decision hiding under it might be whether to promote from inside or hire from outside. Keep pushing past the task until you reach a choice only you can make.

2.  What is the decision that is actually stuck?
Write one sentence. Start it with a verb, such as “Whether to…”, “Which…”, or “How much…”.
Example: Whether to promote Anita from inside or bring in a senior sales head from outside.
3.  How long has it been open, and what is the delay costing?
The time it has stayed open, and the cost of each week of waiting: in money, a missed chance, a person, or a risk you keep carrying.
Example: Open for four months. We lose about two strong candidates a week and the team stays leaderless.
4.  Now say it plainly.
This was never a problem.
It is a decision I have been avoiding.
Example: This was never a staffing problem. It is a promote-or-hire decision I have been avoiding.

Separate Prediction from Judgment

It helps to separate what machines do well from what only a person can be answerable for.

From the previous page · the decision you wrote
How to use

Take the decision you carried over and split it into two parts: the part a machine can work out, and the part only you can own.

Be Mindful!

Prediction is working out what is likely, from facts, patterns and past cases. Machines are cheap and fast at this. Judgment is making the call and being answerable for it. Only a person holds that. If a line you write contains “decide”, “weigh”, or “who is answerable”, it belongs on the judgment side.

1.  What is the prediction inside this decision?
What is mostly about working out what is likely: the facts, scores, forecasts, and first drafts a machine could produce.
Example: Who is likely to hit targets, judged from past performance, test scores, and track record.
2.  What is the judgment inside this decision?
The part only you can own: the trade-off, the final call, and what you would answer for if it went wrong.
Example: Whether I trust this person to hold the team together through a hard year. I will answer for that call.
3.  A quick test.
Imagine a machine handed you a perfect prediction tomorrow. What would still be left for you to decide? Whatever remains is the real judgment.
Example: Even with a perfect ranking, I still have to choose who I can trust with the whole region.
4.  Who decides this today?
Who actually makes the judgment call now. Are they truly attending to it, or quietly leaving it to a tool, a junior, or a default, while keeping the title?
Example: On paper, me. Lately I just approve whoever the shortlist tool puts on top.

Trace the Workflow, Then Choose

The decision sits inside a chain of steps. Map the chain, then decide how far to change it.

From the previous page · the prediction part and the judgment part
How to use

Draw the chain of steps this decision passes through. Then look at it twice: once for a small fix, once for a redesign.

A.  Draw the workflow.
Left to right, from the trigger that starts the work to the final outcome, usually five to nine steps. Put a circle around every step where GenAI could do the work or help with it.
Example: Vacancy raised → JD written → CVs screened → shortlist made → interviews held → offer sent → candidate joins.
Be Mindful!

A small fix gives a clerk a faster calculator: the work still flows the same way. A redesign asks whether you need that step at all, once the calculator does the thinking. The small fix is safe and quick. The redesign is harder and slower, but it is what sets the leaders apart from the ones who only keep up.

The small fix

Where could GenAI make one step faster, with nothing else changing?
Example: Screen and summarise CVs so the recruiter starts from a ready shortlist.

The redesign

If predicting and drafting were free, which steps would you delete, and how would it look built fresh?
Example: Drop the CV-and-shortlist funnel; keep a live model of internal talent the tool maintains.
C.  How much else has to change?
Tick each item that would also have to change for your idea to actually work.
☐  an approval or sign-off step    ☐  another team’s work    ☐  a standard procedure or policy    ☐  who holds authority
Number of ticks:   Zero or one is a small fix · two means run a small trial first · three or more is a redesign.
D.  Compare the two paths.

A small fix

  • Low risk, and easy to undo.
  • A quick but limited gain.
  • Keeps the current structure.
  • Quietly locks in the old way of working.

A redesign

  • Higher risk, and hard to undo.
  • A large gain, but it takes time.
  • Sets up the next generation of the company.
  • Forces the structure to change.

See Where the Power Moves

When the work changes, the hardest part of the job moves, and influence moves with it.

From the previous page · your chosen path
How to use

When the tool takes over the old hard part, the hard part moves somewhere new. Find where it lands, then map who gains and who loses.

1.  What was the old hard part?
What used to be scarce or slow, the thing this workflow was really built around.
Example: Reading every CV and judging fit by hand. That was the slow, scarce skill.
Be Mindful!

When the old bottleneck is removed, the difficulty does not vanish. It moves, usually to judgment, to handling risk, or to coordinating people. Influence follows wherever it lands. Ask not what the tool can do, but what it makes easy, and who now holds the part it cannot.

2.  Where does the hard part move?
Tick one, then describe it for your own workplace.
☐  judgment    ☐  handling risk    ☐  coordinating people
Example: Risk: standing behind who we trust with a region, when a tool can rank but cannot be blamed.

Stage 3, continued

You found where the hard part moved and who rises. Now name the new owner, the likely resistance, and put it together in one sentence.

4.  Who is the new owner?
The role that should now own this process: the one that manages the new hard part and sits closest to the decision.
Example: The Regional HR head, who now owns the trust call and sits closest to the hire.
5.  Who will resist, and what are they protecting?
These are the people, positions, or roles who would resist this change. Write who they are, and what they are really protecting, which is often a committee or a routine that the tool makes unnecessary.
Example: The screening committee, whose standing came from filtering CVs; they will call the tool “risky” to keep the gate.
