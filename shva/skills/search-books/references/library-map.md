## Traps

**The near-miss pair.** Made to Stick and Make It Stick are different books by different authors and both sit in this library. Made to Stick, Chip and Dan Heath, is about why some messages survive retelling. Make It Stick, Brown, Roediger and McDaniel, is learning science on retrieval practice and spacing. Confusing them produces a confident wrong answer. Check which one the question actually needs, and when the question is ambiguous, ask rather than guess.

**Duplicates across stores.** Peripheral Vision, Thinking in Systems and Make It Stick each appear in two stores. Searching both returns the same book twice. Deduplicate before presenting, and pick the store whose framing matches the question.

**Thinking in Systems carries a standing tension.** The curator's `library.md` rules it out as too abstract to operationalize, yet it sits in two stores here and the wiki's reading constitution treats systems passages as a live source of diagnostic analogies. Owning a book is not the same as endorsing it. Retrieve from it when asked, and do not use its presence here as evidence that the ruling-out was wrong.

**Reengineering the Corporation is the carve-out.** The taste profile bans "manifesto" in a subtitle, with one stated exception for the original business process reengineering manifesto as a historical artifact. This is that book. Treat it as historical precedent, which is exactly the use the meta-principle on technology lag prescribes.
